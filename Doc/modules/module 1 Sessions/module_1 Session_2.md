# Why This Session Matters

Your hands naturally follow your eyes.

Where you look determines where the car goes.

One of the biggest differences between beginners and experienced drivers is **not** steering ability—it's vision.

Beginners focus on the road directly in front of the car.

Experienced drivers focus on where they want the car to be **several seconds later**.

Today's lesson teaches you to "drive with your eyes first."

---

# Theory — Look Where You Want to Go

Imagine walking through a doorway.

You don't stare at your feet.

You naturally look toward the doorway, and your body follows.

Driving works exactly the same way.

Your brain continuously plans the car's path using visual information.

If your eyes remain too close to the front bumper:

* Your reactions become late.
* Steering becomes rushed.
* Corners feel tighter than they really are.
* Small mistakes become larger corrections.

Instead, train yourself to look progressively farther ahead.

During every corner:

1. **Approach:** Look toward the braking point.
2. **Turn-In:** Shift your vision toward the apex.
3. **Mid-Corner:** Begin looking toward the exit.
4. **Corner Exit:** Look down the next straight or toward the next corner.

Your eyes should always be leading the car.

> **The car follows your hands.**
>
> **Your hands follow your eyes.**

---

# The Three-Second Rule

As a beginner, try to keep your eyes approximately **three seconds ahead** of the car.

Instead of watching the pavement immediately in front of the hood, focus farther down the track.

Benefits include:

* Earlier steering decisions.
* Smoother corner entry.
* Better racing line.
* More consistent braking.
* Reduced panic corrections.

Looking farther ahead gives your brain more time to process what is coming next.

---

# Practice Drill — Vision Lead Exercise

**Objective:** Train your eyes to stay ahead of the car.

Drive for approximately **10 minutes**.

During every lap:

### On Straights

Look toward the next braking marker.

Avoid watching the road directly ahead.

---

### During Corner Entry

Move your eyes to the apex before turning the wheel.

Do not wait until the car reaches the corner.

---

### Mid-Corner

Shift your focus toward the exit before reaching the apex.

Your steering should naturally become smoother.

---

### Corner Exit

Immediately begin looking toward the next braking point.

Repeat this process for every corner.

Ignore lap time.

Today's goal is to improve visual habits.

---

# Practical Driving Test

Drive **5 consecutive clean laps**.

During each lap, consciously follow this visual sequence:

Braking Point → Apex → Exit → Next Corner

Your objective is to:

* Stay on track.
* Reduce steering corrections.
* Drive smoother than in Session 1.
* Finish each lap without rushing.

If you catch yourself staring directly in front of the car, gently reset your focus and continue.

# Common Beginner Mistakes

### Looking at the Front of the Car

Result:

Late reactions.

Constant steering adjustments.

Instead:

Look toward the next reference point.

---

### Staring at the Apex

Result:

Late corner exits.

Poor preparation for the next section.

Instead:

Once the car is approaching the apex, shift your vision toward the exit.

---

### Looking at Obstacles

Result:

The car often drifts toward the object.

This is known as **target fixation**.

Instead:

Always look toward the safe path—not the hazard.

---

### Moving Only Your Eyes

Result:

Limited field of view.

Instead:

Allow natural head movement while keeping your body relaxed.

# Coach's Notes

Many beginners believe that faster drivers have quicker reactions.

In reality, experienced drivers often appear calm because they are seeing the track **earlier**.

By looking farther ahead, you give yourself more time to think, prepare, and drive smoothly.

Continue practicing the habits from **Session 1**:

* Hands at **9 and 3 o'clock**.
* Relaxed grip.
* One smooth steering input per corner.

Now add today's habit:

**Always let your eyes lead the car.**

In **Session 3**, you'll combine smooth steering and proper vision with the final foundation of fast, consistent driving: **the racing line**.
