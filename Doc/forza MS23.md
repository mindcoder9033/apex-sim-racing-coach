## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Welcome to Sim Racing

Welcome to the world of sim racing. Whether you're here to improve your real-world driving skills, compete online, or simply experience the thrill of racing, Forza Motorsport 2023 is an excellent place to start your journey.

## 2. Getting Started

### 2.1 First Launch

The first time you start Forza Motorsport 2023, you will have the chance to adjust several game settings including driving assists, accessibility options, and graphics settings. Take your time with these—getting them right from the start will save you frustration later.

### 2.2 Game Modes

| Mode | Purpose | Recommended For |
|------|---------|------------------|
| **Builders Cup** | Career mode with structured events | Learning the game |
| **Free Play** | Custom races with any car/track | Practice sessions |
| **Rivals** | Time trials against ghosts | Improving lap times |
| **Featured Multiplayer** | Online ranked racing | Once you're confident |

**Recommendation:** Start with **Free Play** or the **Builders Cup** practice sessions. These allow you to learn at your own pace without the pressure of competition.

### 2.3 The Practice Session

Before every race in the Builders Cup, you'll have a practice session. **Use these.** They are not just for show—they are your opportunity to:

- Learn the track layout
- Find your braking points
- Build muscle memory
- Earn Car XP (CXP) that levels up your car

> *"When you go to a racetrack for the first time, you just have to go there early and drive around it."* — Danny Sullivan, Indy 500 Winner

## 3. Essential Settings

### 3.1 Difficulty Settings

Forza Motorsport offers a range of difficulty presets. As a beginner, you want **enough assistance to learn without frustration, but not so much that the game drives for you.**

| Setting | Beginner Recommendation | Why |
|---------|------------------------|-----|
| **Drivatar Difficulty** | 3-4/10 | Allows you to learn racecraft without being left behind |
| **Damage** | Cosmetic or Fuel & Tire | Learn car behavior without punishing repairs |
| **Fuel & Tire Wear** | On | Learn to manage these from the start |

### 3.2 Track Limits

**Enable Track Limits** in the settings menu under Gameplay & HUD. This feature helps you understand where the track boundaries are—critical for learning the racing line.

### 3.3 Proximity Arrows

**Turn on Proximity Arrows**. These grey arrows appear on screen when another car is near you. They turn yellow when a car is very close. This is especially helpful because Forza Motorsport doesn't have an on-screen radar.

## 4. Driving Assists

### 4.1 The Philosophy

*Going Faster!* teaches that you should learn skills progressively. The same applies to assists:

> *"The most important lesson is that in order to drive extraordinarily well, you have to use your head much more than your guts."* — Going Faster!

**Start with more assists, then gradually remove them as you build confidence.**

### 4.2 Recommended Beginner Settings

| Assist | Beginner Setting | When to Change |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| **Suggested Line** | Full or Braking Only | Turn off when you know the track |
| **Braking** | ABS On | Keep ABS on until you learn threshold braking |
| **Steering** | Normal or Partially Assisted | Switch to Simulation when comfortable |
| **Throttle** | Unassisted | Learn throttle control from the start |
| **Traction Control** | Sport or On | Reduce as you learn throttle control |
| **Stability Control** | On | Turn off when you understand car balance |
| **Shifting** | Automatic | Switch to Manual when comfortable |

### 4.3 The Suggested Line

The Suggested Line is a valuable learning tool:

- **Full:** Shows the entire racing line (blue = accelerate, red = brake)
- **Braking Only:** Shows only braking points

> *"I absolutely use reference points around the track to keep on the line. It gives you consistency."* — Robbie Buhl, IndyCar Driver

**Use the Suggested Line to learn the track, but don't become dependent on it.** Your goal is to develop your own reference points—brake markers, turn-in points, apexes.

### 4.4 When to Remove Assists

| Milestone | Remove This Assist |
|-----------|-------------------|
| You can complete 5 clean laps | Suggested Line → Braking Only |
| You know the track layout | Suggested Line → Off |
| You can brake consistently | ABS → Off |
| You can shift smoothly | Automatic → Manual |
| You can control wheelspin | Traction Control → Off |

**Remove one assist at a time.** Give yourself time to adapt.

## 5. Force Feedback Settings

### 5.1 Why Force Feedback Matters

Force Feedback (FFB) is how the game communicates with you through your wheel. It tells you:

- When the car has grip (heavy wheel)
- When the car is losing grip (light wheel)
- What the road surface feels like
- When you're hitting curbs

> *"When the car is at the limit, the steering wheel will feel heavy and precise. When it goes light, you've lost the front tires."* — Going Faster!

### 5.2 Steering Mode

For a wheel, you have two options:

| Setting | Description | Recommendation |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| **Normal** | Smooths out yaw inertia, more stable | Start here |
| **Simulation** | No assists, more precise but less forgiving | Switch when comfortable |

**Start with Normal.** Switch to Simulation once you're comfortable with car control.

### 5.3 Recommended FFB Settings

These settings are a starting point. Adjust to your preference.

| Setting | Recommended Value | What It Does |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|
| Vibration Scale | 50-60 | Controls vibration intensity |
| Force Feedback Scale | 60-100 | Controls overall FFB strength |
| Steering Self Alignment | 100 | Wheel returns to center naturally |
| Mechanical Trail Scale | 100-140 | Feel grip through suspension |
| Pneumatic Trail Scale | 100 | Feel tire dynamics |
| Road Feel Scale | 130-140 | Feel the track surface |
| Load Sensitivity | 100 | Feel weight transfer |
| Wheel Damping Scale | 30-50 | Smooths oscillations |

**For Direct Drive wheels (like Moza R3):** Start with lower FFB Scale (60-80) and adjust up.

**For Belt/Gear wheels:** Higher FFB Scale (100) may feel better.

### 5.4 Deadzone Settings

Deadzone settings determine how much pedal/steering movement is ignored before input registers.

| Setting | Recommended | Why |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| Steering Deadzone Inside | 0 | Immediate steering response |
| Steering Deadzone Outside | 100 | Full steering range |
| Acceleration Deadzone Inside | 0 | Immediate throttle response |
| Acceleration Deadzone Outside | 95-100 | Full throttle range |
| Deceleration Deadzone Inside | 0 | Immediate brake response |
| Deceleration Deadzone Outside | 95-100 | Full brake range |

### 5.5 What Good FFB Feels Like

| Sensation | What It Means | Your Response |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| **Heavy wheel in corners** | The car has grip | You can trust the car |
| **Wheel goes light** | Front tires losing grip | Reduce steering input |
| **Wheel pulls to one side** | Uneven grip or track camber | Correct smoothly |
| **Rumble/vibration** | Road surface, curbs, bumps | Keep the wheel steady |

## 6. Car Setup Basics

### 6.1 The Philosophy

*Going Faster!* teaches that the car's setup should support your driving, not compensate for poor technique.

> *"The ultimate tweak... is that you are the component that can suddenly pull a one or two percent lap time improvement out of thin air."* — Going Faster!

**Focus on driving skill first. Car setup comes second.**

### 6.2 Where to Find Setup Options

Navigate to **"Cars"** → **"Upgrade & Tune"** → **"Tuning"**. From there, you can adjust:

- Tire pressures
- Fuel load
- Gearing
- Suspension
- Aerodynamics

### 6.3 Simple Setup Adjustments for Beginners

| Adjustment | Effect | Recommendation |
|------------|--------|----------------|
| **Fuel Load** | Less fuel = lighter car = faster | Reduce to minimum + 1 lap reserve |
| **Tire Compound** | Soft = more grip, faster wear | Use Soft for qualifying, Medium for races |
| **Final Drive** | Lower = more acceleration, less top speed | Adjust based on track |

### 6.4 Fuel Load Management

Reducing fuel load is one of the easiest ways to improve lap times:

1. Go to **Car Setup** → **Fuel & Tire**
2. Use the slider to reduce fuel
3. Keep at least **one lap of fuel in reserve**

Less fuel = lighter car = faster acceleration and cornering.

## 7. Practice Routine

### 7.1 The Going Faster! Practice Method

*Going Faster!* teaches a three-step progression:

1. **The Line** → Learn where to drive
2. **Corner Exit** → Learn to carry speed
3. **Braking & Entering** → Learn to slow down efficiently

**Apply this to every practice session.**

### 7.2 Session Structure

| Phase | Duration | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **Warm-up** | 3-5 laps | Learn the track, find reference points |
| **Line Practice** | 5-10 laps | Hit every apex, use all the road |
| **Exit Practice** | 5-10 laps | Smooth throttle, maximize exit speed |
| **Braking Practice** | 5-10 laps | Find brake points, trail-brake |
| **Full Laps** | 5-10 laps | Put it all together |

### 7.3 Using Rivals Mode

**Rivals Mode** is one of the best practice tools in Forza Motorsport. You race against a "ghost" of another driver's best lap.

**How to use Rivals for learning:**

1. Select a car and track you want to practice
2. Choose a rival with a time slightly faster than yours
3. Follow their ghost to learn their line
4. Try to match or beat their time

> *"You can learn what you can from more than one driver as practice sessions go on."* — Going Faster!

### 7.4 The Segment System

Forza Motorsport breaks tracks into segments. Each segment has a target time. This is a powerful learning tool:

- Focus on one segment at a time
- Practice until you hit the target
- Move to the next segment
- Then put it all together

**This mirrors the Going Faster! approach of breaking the track into manageable pieces.**

## 8. The Apex Approach

### 8.1 The Three Fundamentals

*Going Faster!* teaches that there are three basic problems to solve in racing:

1. **Driving on the best path (The Line)**
2. **Carrying speed through corners and onto straights (Corner Exit)**
3. **Efficiently slowing the car at corner entry (Braking & Entering)**

**Every practice session should focus on one of these.**

### 8.2 Applying This to Forza

| Skill | How to Practice in Forza |
|-------|--------------------------|
| **The Line** | Use the Suggested Line, then turn it off. Hit every apex. |
| **Corner Exit** | Focus on smooth throttle. Listen for wheelspin. |
| **Braking** | Find consistent brake points. Learn trail-braking. |

### 8.3 The Reference Point Method

*Going Faster!* emphasizes the importance of reference points:

> *"I absolutely use reference points around the track to keep on the line. It gives you consistency."* — Robbie Buhl

**In Forza, use:**

- Distance boards (100m, 150m)
- Track markings
- Curbs
- Shadows (dynamic, so less reliable)

**For every corner, identify:**

1. **Brake point** → Where you start braking
2. **Turn-in point** → Where you start turning
3. **Apex** → Where you're closest to the inside
4. **Track-out** → Where you exit the corner

### 8.4 The Learning Progression

| Stage | Focus | When to Progress |
|-------|-------|------------------|
| **1. Track Familiarization** | Learn the layout | When you can complete 3 clean laps |
| **2. The Line** | Hit every apex | When you hit 80% of apexes |
| **3. Braking** | Consistent brake points | When you brake at the same point every lap |
| **4. Exit Speed** | Smooth throttle, max exit | When you feel comfortable |
| **5. Full Laps** | Put it all together | When you're consistent |

## 9. Common Beginner Mistakes

### 9.1 Driving Too Fast Too Soon

> *"The key word is 'little.' You'll find that the best bet is to increase your speed a little at a time, not to make a giant leap on faith alone."* — Going Faster!

**In Forza:** Don't try to set a world record on your first lap. Start slow, learn the track, build up speed gradually.

### 9.2 Ignoring Practice Sessions

Practice sessions in the Builders Cup are not optional—they're essential. Use them to:

- Learn the track
- Earn CXP
- Build confidence

### 9.3 Using Too Much Throttle

> *"An abrupt snap off the gas upsets this balance and can steal traction from the rear tires, causing a spin."* — Going Faster!

**In Forza:** Ease up on the throttle before braking. Squeeze the throttle, don't stomp it.

### 9.4 Braking Too Late

> *"Don't be tempted... to go immediately to late-braking when you decide that it's time to go faster."* — Going Faster!

**In Forza:** Brake early and consistently. Move your brake point later only when you're confident.

### 9.5 Forgetting to Look Ahead

> *"The eyes are the single most important partner in driving the race car."* — Terry Earwood, Skip Barber Instructor

**In Forza:** Look at the apex before you turn in. Look at the track-out point before you exit. Your eyes lead the car.

## 10. Troubleshooting

### 10.1 The Car Won't Turn (Understeer)

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Car pushes wide | Too much entry speed | Brake earlier |
| Car won't rotate | Too much throttle | Reduce throttle |
| Front tires feel light | Understeer | Reduce speed, smooth inputs |

### 10.2 The Car Spins (Oversteer)

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Rear slides | Too much throttle | Smoother throttle application |
| Rear slides on lift-off | Abrupt throttle lift | Smoother lift-off |
| Car spins on exit | Power oversteer | Less throttle, countersteer |

### 10.3 Force Feedback Issues

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Wheel too heavy | Reduce Force Feedback Scale |
| Wheel too light | Increase Force Feedback Scale |
| Can't feel the car | Increase Mechanical Trail Scale |
| Can't feel curbs | Increase Vibration Scale and Road Feel |

## 11. Recommended Progression

### Week 1: Foundation

| Session | Focus |
|---------|-------|
| 1 | Set up the game, complete 5 laps at 50% speed |
| 2 | Learn one track (Laguna Seca), find reference points |
| 3 | Practice hitting apexes, ignore lap times |

### Week 2: Building Skills

| Session | Focus |
|---------|-------|
| 1 | Practice braking consistency |
| 2 | Practice corner exits, smooth throttle |
| 3 | Combine braking and corner exit |

### Week 3: Putting It Together

| Session | Focus |
|---------|-------|
| 1 | Full laps, focus on one skill at a time |
| 2 | Rivals Mode, follow a ghost |
| 3 | Full race simulation |

### Week 4: Refining

| Session | Focus |
|---------|-------|
| 1 | Identify weak corners, practice them |
| 2 | Reduce assists (Suggested Line → Braking Only) |
| 3 | Full race, apply everything you've learned |

## 13. Final Thoughts

> *"The real excitement comes at the end of the straightaway where you need to slow the car down to negotiate a corner."* — Going Faster!

Sim racing is a journey. You won't be fast on day one. You won't master every skill in week one. But with consistent practice, the right settings, and a focus on fundamentals, you will improve.

**Remember:**

1. **Start with the right settings** — They make learning easier
2. **Use the Suggested Line to learn** — Then turn it off
3. **Practice one skill at a time** — Don't try to do everything at once
4. **Be patient with yourself** — Improvement takes time
5. **Have fun** — That's why you're here

> *"The key is to have a plan for what you are going to do."* — Going Faster!

Now you have a plan. Go execute it.
