# Why This Session Matters

A racing line is not about driving the shortest distance.

It is about driving the **fastest and smoothest path** through a corner while maintaining the greatest possible grip.

Beginners often steer directly toward the inside of the corner, forcing themselves into a tight turn and reducing exit speed.

Experienced drivers use the entire width of the circuit to create a larger turning radius, allowing the car to remain balanced and carry more speed.

Today's goal is not to memorize a specific track.

Today's goal is to understand **why every corner has an ideal path**.

Once you understand the principle, you can apply it to any circuit.

---

# Theory — The Three Phases of Every Corner

Every corner, regardless of its shape or speed, can be divided into three phases.

## 1. Corner Entry

This is where you prepare the car for turning.

During entry:

* Position the car toward the outside of the track.
* Finish most of your braking before turning.
* Look toward the apex.
* Begin steering smoothly.

A calm, controlled entry creates a stable corner.

---

## 2. Apex

The apex is the point where your car comes closest to the inside edge of the corner.

It is **not** always the geometric center of the turn.

A proper apex allows you to:

* Rotate the car efficiently.
* Maintain balance.
* Prepare for acceleration.

Do not aim for the apex too early.

A late apex is often safer and produces a better corner exit.

---

## 3. Corner Exit

After passing the apex:

* Begin unwinding the steering wheel.
* Allow the car to use the full width of the track.
* Apply throttle progressively.
* Look toward the next corner.

A fast exit is almost always more valuable than a fast entry.

Remember:

> **Slow in. Smooth through. Fast out.**

---

# The Outside–Inside–Outside Principle

The basic racing line follows a simple pattern:

1. **Outside** – Approach the corner from the outside edge of the track.
2. **Inside** – Clip the apex with a smooth steering input.
3. **Outside** – Allow the car to naturally drift back toward the outside on corner exit.

Using the full width of the track increases the corner's effective radius, reducing the amount of steering required and helping the tires maintain grip.

The goal is not to touch every curb. The goal is to make every corner as smooth and repeatable as possible.

---

# Practice Drill — Racing Line Exercise

**Objective:** Drive every corner using the Outside–Inside–Outside technique.

Drive for approximately **10 minutes**.

For each corner:

1. Position the car on the outside of the track.
2. Look toward the apex.
3. Turn the wheel once with a smooth, deliberate input.
4. Clip the apex without making extra steering corrections.
5. Gradually unwind the wheel and let the car move back toward the outside edge on exit.

Drive at **70–80% effort**.

Ignore lap times.

Concentrate on building a repeatable rhythm.

---

# Practical Driving Test

Drive **5 consecutive clean laps**.

During each lap, focus on:

* Using the full width of the circuit.
* Following the same racing line every lap.
* Looking ahead from braking point to apex to exit.
* Maintaining smooth steering.

Your goal is **consistency**, not speed.

If you miss an apex or leave the circuit, reset your focus and continue.

# Session Challenge

## The "Repeat the Line" Challenge

Choose one medium-speed corner.

Drive through it **five consecutive times**.

After each attempt, ask yourself:

* Did I enter from the outside?
* Did I clip the apex smoothly?
* Did I allow the car to exit naturally to the outside?

Your objective is to make each attempt look and feel nearly identical.

Consistency is the challenge—not outright pace.

# Common Beginner Mistakes

### Turning in Too Early

Result:

* Tight corner exit.
* Early understeer.
* Reduced exit speed.

Instead:

Be patient.

Allow the corner to come to you before turning.

---

### Ignoring the Full Width of the Track

Result:

* Sharper steering angles.
* Less grip.
* Reduced stability.

Instead:

Use as much legal track width as possible.

---

### Looking at the Apex for Too Long

Result:

Late preparation for the exit.

Instead:

As you approach the apex, shift your vision toward the exit.

---

### Steering More After the Apex

Result:

Unstable corner exits.

Instead:

Begin unwinding the steering wheel once the car is pointed toward the exit.
