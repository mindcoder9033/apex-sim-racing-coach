### Session 3.1: Throttle Application

**Day 1 | Focus: The "Squeeze" technique**
**Car: Mazda MX-5 | Track: Laguna Seca | Target: 1:36.0**

---

#### THEORY (10 min)

**The Art of Throttle Application**

Throttle application is not an on/off switch. It's a progressive squeeze that builds from nothing to full power.

**The "Squeeze" Concept:**

Imagine pressing a ripe tomato—you want to apply pressure gradually, not crush it. The same applies to the throttle.

```
Abrupt Throttle:   ────╱████████ (full throttle instantly)
Smooth Throttle:   ────╭──╮──╮──╮ (progressive build)
```

**Why Smooth Matters:**

| Input | Result |
|-------|--------|
| **Smooth squeeze** | Weight transfers progressively, car stays balanced |
| **Abrupt stomp** | Weight transfers violently, tires lose grip |

**The Throttle Application Timeline:**

```
Throttle Application Point (Apex/Exit)
    │
    ▼
0% ──────────────────────────────────────► 100%
    │                                      │
    │─── Progressive Squeeze ───►          │
    │   (Building power gradually)         │
```

**Key Principle:**

> *"The more smoothly you apply throttle, the more grip you have available for cornering."*

**Key Takeaways:**
- ✅ Throttle is squeezed, not stomped
- ✅ Smoothness = more grip
- ✅ Progress is key

### Session 3.2: Managing Wheelspin

**Day 3 | Focus: Traction control with your foot**
**Car: Mazda MX-5 | Track: Laguna Seca | Target: 1:35.5**

---

#### THEORY (10 min)

**The Enemy of Exit Speed**

Wheelspin is the enemy of acceleration. When the tires spin, you're not moving forward as fast as you could be.

**What is Wheelspin?**

When the driving wheels spin faster than the car's speed, the tires lose grip. This is wheelspin.

```
Car speed: 40 mph
Wheel speed: 55 mph
Wheelspin: YES
```

**Why Wheelspin is Bad:**

| Effect | Why It Hurts |
|--------|--------------|
| **Lost grip** | You're not accelerating as fast as possible |
| **Wasted power** | Power is being used to spin tires, not move car |
| **Tire wear** | Spinning tires wear out faster |
| **Lost control** | Spinning tires can cause oversteer |

**The Optimal Slip:**

A small amount of wheelspin (5-10%) can be optimal. It allows the tire to generate maximum grip. Too much slip = lost time.

**Managing Wheelspin:**

1. **Squeeze the throttle** (don't stomp)
2. **Feel the tires** (listen for spin)
3. **Modulate pressure** (reduce if spinning)
4. **Build progressively** (increase as grip allows)

> *"The mark of a good driver is the ability to apply the throttle without spinning the tires."* — Going Faster!

**Key Takeaways:**
- ✅ Wheelspin = lost time
- ✅ Small slip = okay
- ✅ Modulate to stay at the limit

### Session 3.3: Power Understeer

**Day 5 | Focus: Recognizing and correcting power understeer**
**Car: Mazda MX-5 | Track: Laguna Seca | Target: 1:35.0**

---

#### THEORY (10 min)

**When Throttle Causes Understeer**

Power understeer occurs when you apply throttle while cornering and the front tires lose grip.

**What is Power Understeer?**

When you apply throttle mid-corner, weight transfers to the rear. This unloads the front tires, reducing their grip. The car "pushes" wide.

```
Throttle Application
        │
        ▼
Weight transfers rear
        │
        ▼
Front tires unload
        │
        ▼
Front grip decreases
        │
        ▼
UNDERSTEER
```

**Symptoms of Power Understeer:**

- The car runs wide on exit
- You have to add more steering lock
- The front tires feel vague
- The car doesn't rotate as you want

**How to Fix Power Understeer:**

1. **Reduce throttle slightly** (transfers weight forward)
2. **Wait for the front to grip**
3. **Reapply throttle progressively**
4. **Steer less** (more steering makes it worse)

> *"Dealing with understeer is done by adjusting the throttle, not the steering wheel."* — Going Faster!

**Key Takeaways:**
- ✅ Power understeer = too much throttle
- ✅ Solution = reduce throttle
- ✅ Steering more doesn't help

### Session 3.4: Power Oversteer

**Day 8 | Focus: Recognizing and correcting power oversteer**
**Car: Mazda MX-5 | Track: Laguna Seca | Target: 1:34.5**

---

#### THEORY (10 min)

**When Throttle Causes Oversteer**

Power oversteer occurs when you apply throttle and the rear tires lose grip. The car's tail slides wide.

**What is Power Oversteer?**

When you apply too much throttle, the rear tires spin. The rear end loses grip and slides toward the outside of the corner.

```
Too Much Throttle
        │
        ▼
Rear tires spin
        │
        ▼
Rear grip decreases
        │
        ▼
Rear slides wide
        │
        ▼
OVERSTEER
```

**Symptoms of Power Oversteer:**

- The rear of the car slides wide
- You have to countersteer
- The car rotates more than you want
- You feel like you might spin

**How to Fix Power Oversteer:**

1. **Countersteer** (steer into the slide)
2. **Reduce throttle slightly** (transfers weight forward)
3. **Wait for rear grip** (the slide stabilizes)
4. **Reapply throttle smoothly**

**The Correction-Pause-Recovery Technique:**

```
Correction: Steer into the slide
    │
    ▼
Pause: The slide stops
    │
    ▼
Recovery: Bring the wheel back straight
```

> *"Oversteer is corrected with the steering wheel, not the throttle."* — Going Faster!

**Key Takeaways:**
- ✅ Power oversteer = too much throttle
- ✅ Solution = countersteer + reduce throttle
- ✅ Smoothness is key

**Car: Mazda MX-5 | Track: Laguna Seca | Target: 1:34.0**

#### THEORY (10 min)

**The Throttle as a Steering Tool**

Your throttle is more than an accelerator—it's a steering tool. By modulating throttle, you can control the car's rotation.

**How Throttle Affects Rotation:**

| Throttle Input | Effect | Result |
|----------------|--------|--------|
| **More throttle** | Rear grip decreases | More rotation (oversteer) |
| **Less throttle** | Rear grip increases | Less rotation (understeer) |

**Throttle Steering in Action:**

```
Corner Entry:      Light throttle → Stabilizes the car
Mid-Corner:        Modulate throttle → Control rotation
Corner Exit:       Progressive squeeze → Maximum exit speed
```

**The Art of Throttle Steering:**

1. **Entry:** Light throttle keeps the car balanced
2. **Mid-corner:** Modulate to control yaw angle
3. **Exit:** Progressive squeeze for maximum speed

> *"The throttle is not just for acceleration—it's a steering wheel for the rear tires."* — Going Faster!

**Key Takeaways:**
- ✅ Throttle controls rotation
- ✅ More throttle = more rotation
- ✅ Less throttle = less rotation

#### THEORY (10 min)

**The Art of Balance**

Car balance is the relationship between front and rear grip. A balanced car is fast and predictable.

**What is Car Balance?**

Car balance is the distribution of grip between front and rear tires. When the car is balanced, both ends have equal grip.

```
Front grip ──────────────────────────────── Rear grip
                    │
                    │ Balanced
                    │ (equal grip)
```

**How Inputs Affect Balance:**

| Input | Effect on Balance |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Brake** | Transfers weight forward | Increases front grip |
| **Throttle** | Transfers weight rearward | Increases rear grip |
| **Steering** | Transfers weight laterally | Affects cornering grip |

**Managing Balance:**

1. **Smooth inputs** (weight transfers progressively)

3. **Feel the balance** (sense what the car is doing)

> *"A balanced car is a fast car."* — Going Faster!
