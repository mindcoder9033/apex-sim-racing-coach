# Why This Session Matters

Learning **when** to brake is only half of the skill.

Learning **how** to release the brake is equally important.

Many beginners press the brake correctly but then suddenly lift their foot off the pedal. This rapid release causes the car's weight to shift rearward too quickly, reducing front tire grip just as the car begins to turn.

The result is often:

* Understeer.
* Wide corner exits.
* Extra steering corrections.
* Reduced confidence.

Today's goal is to make every brake release **smooth and predictable**.

---

# Theory — Understanding Weight Transfer

A car's weight is constantly moving.

Even though the car's total weight never changes, the amount of load carried by each tire changes whenever you:

* Brake.
* Accelerate.
* Turn.
* Change direction.

During braking:

* Weight shifts toward the **front** tires.
* Front tires gain grip.
* Rear tires become lighter.

As you release the brake:

* Weight gradually moves back toward the center of the car.

If this happens smoothly, the car remains balanced.

If it happens suddenly, the car becomes unsettled.

Your goal is to **manage** weight transfer rather than react to it.

Remember:

> **Smooth inputs create smooth weight transfer.**

---

# The "Squeeze–Hold–Release" Technique

Think of every braking zone as having three stages.

### 1. Squeeze

Apply the brake firmly but progressively.

Avoid stamping on the pedal.

---

### 2. Hold

Maintain steady pressure while slowing the car.

Allow the vehicle to settle.

---

### 3. Release

Gradually reduce brake pressure before steering.

Imagine slowly lifting your foot rather than suddenly letting go.

The smoother your release, the more stable the front tires remain as you begin turning.

---

# Practice Drill — Progressive Brake Release

**Objective:** Learn to release the brake smoothly before turning.

Drive for approximately **10 minutes**.

For one chosen corner:

1. Brake at your normal marker.

2. Apply firm, consistent pressure.

3. Count silently:

   * "Brake..."
   * "Hold..."
   * "Release..."

4. Finish releasing the brake just before turning into the corner.

5. Notice how the car feels compared to an abrupt release.

Repeat until the sequence feels natural.

Ignore lap times.

Today's goal is vehicle balance.

---

# Practical Driving Test

Drive **5 consecutive clean laps**.

During each lap:

* Use the same braking marker.
* Apply progressive brake pressure.
* Release the brake smoothly before steering.
* Enter the corner without abrupt weight shifts.
* Maintain a smooth racing line.

The objective is not to brake later—it is to make every corner entry calm and repeatable.

# Session Challenge

## The "Silent Pedal" Challenge

Imagine there is a glass of water resting on the dashboard.

Your task is to brake and release the pedal so smoothly that the water never spills.

After each braking zone, ask yourself:

* Was my brake release gradual?
* Did the car remain stable?
* Did I need extra steering corrections?

If the answer is "Yes," you're developing good brake release habits.

# Common Beginner Mistakes

### Suddenly Releasing the Brake

Result:

* Abrupt rearward weight transfer.
* Reduced front grip.
* Understeer.

Instead:

Lift your foot progressively.

---

### Holding the Brake Too Long

Result:

* Turning while braking heavily.
* Reduced steering grip.
* Wide corner exits.

Instead:

Finish most braking before beginning the turn.

---

### Rushing Into the Corner

Result:

* Unstable vehicle.
* Panic steering.
* Missed apexes.

Instead:

Be patient.

Allow the car to settle before turning.

---

### Focusing Only on the Brake Pedal

Result:

Drivers forget vision and steering.

Instead:

Continue using the complete sequence:

**Vision → Braking → Brake Release → Steering → Exit**

# Coach's Notes

Today's session introduced one of the most important concepts in driving dynamics:

> **The car responds to how smoothly you move its weight.**

You are beginning to understand that every control input—steering, braking, and throttle—changes the balance of the car.

At this stage, don't worry about advanced techniques like **trail braking**. Those require precise control and will be introduced in Module 2 after you've built consistent fundamentals.

For now, continue practicing this sequence on every corner:

1. Look ahead.
2. Brake at a consistent marker.
3. Release the brake smoothly.
4. Turn with one deliberate steering input.
5. Follow the racing line.

In **Session 6**, you'll combine everything you've learned so far while focusing on **Apex Selection & Corner Exit**, where you'll discover why a patient corner entry often leads to a faster and more controlled exit.
