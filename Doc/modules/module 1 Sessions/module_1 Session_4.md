# Why This Session Matters

Many beginners believe faster drivers brake later.

Professional drivers brake **more consistently**, not necessarily later.

A good braking zone creates everything that follows:

* Stable corner entry.
* Predictable steering.
* Better racing line.
* Earlier throttle application.
* Faster corner exits.

Poor braking forces the driver to react throughout the corner.

Good braking allows the driver to plan the entire corner before turning the wheel.

Today's goal is not to brake as late as possible.

Today's goal is to brake **at exactly the same place, every lap.**

---

# Theory — Braking Before Turning

Every corner begins with braking.

The purpose of braking is not simply to reduce speed—it is to prepare the car for turning.

As you press the brake pedal, the car's weight shifts forward.

This increases grip at the front tires, allowing them to steer effectively.

However, turning while still applying heavy brake pressure overloads the front tires and often causes the car to understeer.

For Module 1, follow a simple sequence:

1. Identify your braking point.
2. Brake firmly in a straight line.
3. Release most of the brake pressure before turning.
4. Turn the wheel smoothly.
5. Accelerate only after the car is pointed toward the exit.

Advanced techniques such as trail braking will be introduced in later modules.

For now, focus on building a repeatable routine.

---

# Choosing a Braking Point

A braking point is a visual reference that tells you where to begin slowing the car.

Examples include:

* Distance boards.
* Start of curbing.
* Painted track markings.
* Overhead bridges.
* Marshal posts.
* Shadows that remain consistent.

Choose **one fixed reference** and use it every lap.

Avoid guessing.

If your braking point changes every lap, your corner entry will never become consistent.

---

# Practice Drill — Brake Marker Exercise

**Objective:** Learn to brake at the same location every lap.

Drive for approximately **10 minutes**.

For one selected corner:

1. Choose a fixed braking marker.
2. Approach at the same speed each lap.
3. Apply firm, smooth brake pressure.
4. Complete braking before steering.
5. Evaluate whether the braking point was too early, too late, or correct.

Repeat until your braking becomes repeatable.

Ignore lap time.

Today's goal is consistency.

---

# Practical Driving Test

Drive **5 consecutive clean laps**.

During each lap:

* Brake at the same marker.
* Brake in a straight line.
* Complete braking before turning.
* Stay on the racing surface.
* Avoid locking the brakes or running wide.

Your success is measured by how repeatable your braking becomes.

# Session Challenge

## The "Same Marker Every Lap" Challenge

Choose one corner.

For **five consecutive laps**, brake at exactly the same reference point.

After each lap, ask yourself:

* Did I brake at the correct marker?
* Was my brake pressure smooth?
* Did I finish braking before steering?

The goal is to make all five braking zones feel identical.

# Common Beginner Mistakes

### Braking Too Late

Result:

* Missed apexes.
* Running wide.
* Panic steering.
* Poor corner exits.

Instead:

Brake earlier than you think you need to.

Consistency is more valuable than bravery.

---

### Stabbing the Brake Pedal

Result:

* Abrupt weight transfer.
* Reduced stability.
* Unsettled vehicle.

Instead:

Press the brake pedal firmly but progressively.

Imagine squeezing the pedal rather than stamping on it.

---

### Looking at the Brake Pedal

Result:

Loss of visual focus.

Instead:

Keep your eyes on the braking marker and the corner ahead.

Trust your feet.

---

### Turning Before Finishing Braking

Result:

Understeer.

Reduced front tire grip.

Wide corner exits.

Instead:

Separate braking and steering.

Brake first.

Turn second.

A driver who always brakes at the correct point will improve faster than a driver who constantly experiments with late braking.

# Coach's Notes

Welcome to **Phase 2 – Build Control**.

Today's lesson introduced one of the most important principles in performance driving:

> **The quality of your corner is determined before you begin turning.**

Continue applying the skills from the previous sessions:

* Smooth steering.
* Looking ahead.
* Following a repeatable racing line.

Now add one new habit:

**Brake at the same point, the same way, every lap.**

Do not worry if you feel slower than before. Consistent braking often requires braking slightly earlier while you build confidence. As your technique improves, your braking points will naturally become more efficient.

In **Session 5**, you'll learn how to **release the brake smoothly** and understand how brake release affects the car's balance through corner entry—a key stepping stone toward advanced techniques like trail braking.
